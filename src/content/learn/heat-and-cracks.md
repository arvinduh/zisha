---
title: Heat, cold, and cracks
hanzi: 温壶
---

Clay expands as it heats. If one part of the pot gets hot much faster than the
rest, as when boiling water hits a cold pot, the strain can open a hairline
crack. Nearly every cracked zisha pot comes down to this, or to a dropped lid.

### Warm it in stages

Zhuni is the clay most sensitive to this, so its makers say to warm it in
stages every time, even in summer: lukewarm water first, then hot, then the
tea. The exact steps open every [brew](/tea/bai-mudan/#before).

### Hidden cold

A room can feel warm while the pot isn't. A pot stored by a window, against an
outside wall, or in a cold cupboard can sit near 10 °C. Stone, slate, and iron
trays also pull heat out of the base. Use a bamboo, cork, or cloth mat under
the pot.

### Never

- Heat the pot over a flame, on a stove, or in a microwave.
- Warm it up with a hair dryer.
- Put it in the fridge, the freezer, or the dishwasher.

These all heat or cool one spot much faster than the rest.

### Checking for a crack

- **Tap test.** With the lid off and the pot empty, tap the shoulder gently
  with a fingernail. A clear ring is healthy. A dull, flat sound suggests a
  hidden crack.
- **Leak test.** Fill with room-temperature water, cover the spout, put the lid
  on, and blow gently into the lid's air hole. Watch where the handle and spout
  join the body, and the lower walls, for beads of water.

If it fails, stop using it. Hot water makes cracks grow. A cracked pot can be
repaired with traditional metal staples or kintsugi (lacquer and gold), and
many people love a mended pot even more.

### The lid

Lids sit loose by design and are the part most often broken. Set it down on a
cloth rather than on stone, and keep a fingernail on it when you pour (see
[the rules](/care/daily/#rules)).
