---
title: What makes zisha special
hanzi: 紫砂
---

Zisha (紫砂, "purple sand") is the family of clays mined around Yixing, near Lake Tai in
Jiangsu province. Potters there have made teapots from it for about five
hundred years.

### A pot that breathes

Zisha is fired without glaze, and its structure is full of microscopic pores.
Three things follow from that:

- **It holds heat steadily** and releases it slowly, so a brew stays at an even
  temperature.
- **It's slightly absorbent.** Each session leaves a trace of tea oils in the
  clay, and over the years the pot starts to season the tea itself.
- **It takes in anything.** That means soap, grease, smells, and mold too,
  which is where every care rule comes from.

### One pot, one tea

Because the clay keeps a memory of what you brew, each pot is dedicated to one
tea, or at most one closely related family. Brew different teas in the same pot
and their traces mix, which flattens both. Some teas can share a pot because
they're processed alike, such as different roasted rock oolongs, or different
aged, pressed white teas. A fresh, floral tea and a dark, fermented one should
never share.

If a pot ever needs to switch teas, it needs a
[full reset](/care/fix/#wrong-tea). Mostly, pick its tea once and keep it.

### Your clay: zhuni

Different ores give different clays. Yours is **zhuni** 朱泥, "vermilion
clay": iron-rich, fired dense, and bright red-orange. It shrinks a lot in the
kiln, so it's hard to work, and zhuni pots tend to be small and thin-walled.

- **It holds heat hard**, which pushes aroma up into the cup. With a delicate
  tea that means brewing a little cooler and lifting the lid between steeps.
- **It absorbs the least** of the zisha clays, so it's the most forgiving of
  one pot, one tea, and keeps light teas tasting clean.
- **It's the most sensitive to sudden heat.** Always warm it in two stages.
- **Its patina comes fastest**: a glassy luster within weeks.

For comparison, the other common clays:

- **Zini** 紫泥, purple-brown. Ripe pu-erh, black tea, roasted oolong. Patina
  over months.
- **Duanni** 段泥, sandy beige. Green, white, light oolong. Shows stains most
  easily; patina over a year or more.
- **Lüni** 绿泥, pale green-grey. Delicate greens, Silver Needle.
- **Dicaoqing** 底槽清, deep speckled purple. Aged pu-erh, premium rock
  oolong.

### Signs of a real pot

Iron specks, a little mica sparkle, small tool marks, and a lid that wobbles
slightly are all normal for handmade zisha, not defects.
